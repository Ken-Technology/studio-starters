01

In your industry

The integrator sells the robot. Nobody sells the second year

When a machine goes in through an integrator, the plant's relationship is with the integrator. The builder meets the customer at the install and then mostly at a breakdown.

Your site now lists a service plan with remote monitoring, which is the first thing a plant hears from you after the install that is not a repair. How that plan is offered matters as much as what is in it. If it arrives as a line on the integrator's quote, it gets cut first.

The builders who keep plants on a plan treat the handover at install as the start of their own relationship, with their own name on it.

02

One idea for Brightline Robotics

Put a renewal date on every installed robot

Most service plans lapse because nobody saw the date coming. A renewal calendar is unglamorous and it works.

List every robot your service team has installed, with the plant, the integrator, the install date and whether it is on a plan. Put a renewal date on each, even the ones with no plan today, and give every plant one owner on your side.

Then, ninety days before each date, that owner sends one short note with the machine's monitoring data from the last quarter. A plant that sees its own numbers rarely lets the plan lapse.

Where to start

Export the install list from your service team and add two columns: plan status and renewal date.
