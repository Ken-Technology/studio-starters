In your industry

Night shifts are where cold chain breaks go unnoticed

Most produce distributors run their busiest receiving hours after dark, and that is also when the fewest experienced eyes are on the dock. A pallet that sits too long at the wrong temperature looks fine at night and shows up as a rejected load later in the week.

Your site describes three cold storage warehouses and your own refrigerated fleet, which puts more of that handoff under your control than most of your peers have. It also means every gap in a night shift is yours to find.

The teams that handle this well do not add more sensors first. They give the night lead one short rule and one person to call when a reading drifts.

02

One idea for Coastline Produce

Give the night lead a one-page temperature exception loop

Right now a drifting reading on a night shift probably waits for the morning. The fix is not a new system. It is a written rule for what a lead does in the next ten minutes when a reefer or a room reads out of range: who they call, what they move, and what they write down.

Start with one warehouse and one week. Keep the sheet to a single page and put it where the lead stands, not in a shared drive. At the end of the week, read every entry with the lead who wrote it.

If the loop works on paper, it is easy to move into whatever tool you already use. If it does not, no tool would have saved it.

Where to start

Pick one Tacoma warehouse and write the out-of-range rule for its night lead on one page before the new hires start.
