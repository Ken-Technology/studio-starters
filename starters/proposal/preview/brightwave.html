1The problem in Brightwave Logistics' words

These are the three things we heard on Oct 14 and Oct 21, as close to how they were said as our notes allow.

We find out a load slipped when the customer calls. By then there is nothing left to do but apologize.

Leo Marsh, VP Operations, Oct 14

Every terminal keeps its own handoff sheet. A Milwaukee to Indianapolis load crosses three spreadsheets and nobody owns the middle.

Dana Whitfield, Director of Dispatch, Oct 14

I am hiring four coordinators and I cannot tell you which terminal needs them most.

What it costs today

From Dana's September exception log for Joliet, shared Oct 23.

140late-load calls at Joliet in September
58 hof dispatch and service time on those calls, at 25 minutes each
6 of 12dispatchers worked at least one Saturday to clear the backlog

None of this is a people problem. The information exists in the TMS and on the terminal sheets. It reaches the wrong person, or reaches the right one too late.

2What we propose

One exception board and one handoff timeline for every load, starting at Joliet. The platform reads from your TMS; nothing is written back.

In scope for the pilot

  • Read-only connector to the TMS, inbound and outbound lanes at Joliet
  • Exception board for the 12 Joliet dispatchers, with reason codes and next action
  • Handoff timeline per load, from pickup to proof of delivery
  • Single sign-on through Brightwave's Microsoft Entra tenant
  • Weekly 30-minute review with Dana and Jordan

Out of scope until year one

  • The other six terminals
  • Customer-facing tracking pages
  • Carrier invoicing and settlement
  • Capacity planning view across lanes (built on pilot data, delivered in year one)

If the pilot passes, year one extends the same setup to all seven terminals, two a week, with the Joliet team training the next site.
