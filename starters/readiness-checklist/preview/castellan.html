The checklist

10 items, in the order that matters most. We ticked what we could see from outside, so fix anything we got wrong.

Close these first

5 gaps, in the order to close them. Each one comes with a first step you can take this week.

  1. An executive sponsor who owns the outcome

    First step: Write down who decides when scope and timeline collide. If it takes more than a minute, that is the gap.

  2. A project manager with the time to run it

    First step: Fill the role or free up half of someone's week for the next year. Part time project managers slip go-lives.

  3. One item master across plants

    First step: Pick 50 parts both plants buy and compare their records. The differences show the cleanup ahead.

  4. Inventory counts you trust

    First step: Count one storeroom this month and compare it to what the spreadsheet says. Keep the difference as your baseline.

  5. Processes written as they run today

    First step: Walk one customer order from quote to invoice and write down every hand-off, including the workarounds.

  6. Shop floor data captured at the machine

    First step: List the stations where paper still travels to the office. Each one is a data feed the new system will not have.

  7. Both plants on one network and one login

    First step: Check whether a planner from one plant can log in at the other without a workaround.

  8. Budget and timeline approved

    First step: Get the approval on paper, with the go-live window and the projects that pause for it.

  9. Super users named in each plant

    First step: Ask each plant manager for two names and a day a week from January. The answer tells you how ready they are.

  10. Sized for the next plant

    First step: Write the list of what a new plant needs on day one: sites, warehouses, work centers, roles. Keep it to one page.

Everything on this list is in place. If that holds up on a closer look, Castellan Machine Works is ready, and the call below is a short one.
