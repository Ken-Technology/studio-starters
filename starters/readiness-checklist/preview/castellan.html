The checklist

10 items, in the order that matters most. We ticked what we could see from outside, so fix anything we got wrong.

Close these first

5 gaps, in the order to close them. Each one comes with a first step you can take this week.

  1. An executive sponsor who owns the outcome

    First stepWrite down who decides when scope and timeline collide. If it takes more than a minute, that is the gap.

  2. A project manager with the time to run it

    First stepFill the role or free up half of someone's week for the next year. Part time project managers slip go-lives.

  3. One item master across plants

    First stepPick 50 parts both plants buy and compare their records. The differences show the cleanup ahead.

  4. Inventory counts you trust

    First stepCount one storeroom this month and compare it to what the spreadsheet says. Keep the difference as your baseline.

  5. Processes written as they run today

    First stepWalk one customer order from quote to invoice and write down every hand-off, including the workarounds.

  6. Shop floor data captured at the machine

    First stepList the stations where paper still travels to the office. Each one is a data feed the new system will not have.

  7. Both plants on one network and one login

    First stepCheck whether a planner from one plant can log in at the other without a workaround.

  8. Budget and timeline approved

    First stepGet the approval on paper, with the go-live window and the projects that pause for it.

  9. Super users named in each plant

    First stepAsk each plant manager for two names and a day a week from January. The answer tells you how ready they are.

  10. Sized for the next plant

    First stepWrite the list of what a new plant needs on day one: sites, warehouses, work centers, roles. Keep it to one page.

Everything on this list is in place. If that holds up on a closer look, Castellan Machine Works is ready, and the call below is a short one.
