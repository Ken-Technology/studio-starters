  4. Our notes on Cobalt Fleet Software

    Cobalt Fleet Software sells preventive maintenance software to trucking fleets of 50 to 500 trucks. It reads engine fault codes from telematics and books shop time before a truck fails on the road. About 90 people, based in Columbus. Released a new telematics integration in October and posted two sales development roles.

What we made

4 parts, in the order to read them. Each one stands on its own, so take what is useful and leave the rest.

  1. Go after the shop, not the fleet owner

    The bet

    Your site says the shop sees a day plan instead of a pile of alerts. That line is written for the person who runs the shop, so the first segment is maintenance directors, not owners or finance. The ask is small: would they like to see what Cobalt does with one week of their own fault codes. Fleets of 50 to 500 trucks is your own number, so the list stays inside it.

  2. Three kinds of company, one role in each

    Who to reach

    All three come from the who uses it line on your site. In each one the list targets the person who owns shop time and downtime, by title.

    • Director of maintenance at regional truckload carriersFleets of 50 to 500 trucks, the size your site names. Owns the downtime number.
    • VP of operations at food distributors with their own fleetRefrigerated routes run on a fixed schedule. A truck down is a route missed.
    • Fleet manager at waste and recycling haulersThe same preventive maintenance problem, on trucks that run the same route every day.

  3. Under 90 words, one fact, one ask

    The first email

    The greeting and the name are added per person. The subject line is the first two words of the plan, shop day plan, because it reads like a note from inside their own company.

    If fault codes still land in an inbox and the shop sorts them on Monday, a truck that could have had a shop date fails on the road instead.

    Cobalt reads the codes from your telematics and sets a shop date per truck, so the shop gets a day plan instead of a pile of alerts. It is built for fleets of 50 to 500 trucks.

    Want me to show you what it does with one week of your own codes?

  4. The first two weeks

    Days 1 to 14

    The schedule runs the way every first month runs with us, cut to the part before the first follow-up.

    • Days 1 to 3Agree the segment and the ask. Build the list and check every company against the 50 to 500 truck range.
    • Days 4 to 5Sending domains set up and warmed apart from cobaltfleet.example, so your main domain stays clean.
    • Days 6 to 10The first send goes out under your name. Every reply answered within a working day.
    • Days 11 to 14The first follow-up. Friday readout: what was sent, who answered and what they said.

What the full version includes

This is the first two weeks of a month. The full month adds the list, the follow-ups and the replies, then a new segment the month after.

  1. The list, built and checked

    Every maintenance director at the carriers in the segment, checked by us before the first send.

  2. Three follow-ups and a LinkedIn note

    Each on a different reason to answer, spaced over three weeks.

  3. Replies answered, meetings booked

    We answer every reply within a working day and put meetings straight on your calendar.

  4. A readout every Friday

    What was sent, who answered and what they said, so you can steer the next segment.

  5. A new segment each month

    Food distributors or waste haulers next, from the same who uses it line on your site.
