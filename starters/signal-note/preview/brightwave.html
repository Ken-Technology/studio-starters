Three things we noticed about Brightwave Logistics this month

For Leo Marsh, VP Operations at Brightwave Logistics

Leo,

Each month I write down what changed at a few companies I follow, and what it usually means. This one is about Brightwave Logistics. Three things stood out.

  1. 1 Hiring Seen October 24

    Brightwave is hiring six dispatch coordinators

    Brightwave's careers page lists six dispatch coordinator roles, four in Chicago and two in Dallas. The posting was seen on October 24.1

    What it usually means. A company adds dispatch headcount when volume grows faster than the process that handles it. Six at once often means the current team is covering with overtime, and new hires take longer to reach full speed when the work lives in people's heads rather than in a system.

    One idea. Before the six start, write down the ten decisions a coordinator makes most often, one page each. It shortens the ramp, and it shows which steps a system could take over.

  2. 2 News Seen October 14

    A second hub opened in Dallas

    Brightwave opened a cross-dock in Dallas, its first hub outside Chicago, in the middle of October.2

    What it usually means. A second hub doubles the handoffs: two dispatch desks, two sets of carrier relationships, and customers who expect one answer. Companies often run the new site on the first site's playbook for months before they find out which parts did not travel.

    One idea. Pick three numbers both hubs report the same way from day one, such as on-time pickup, dock dwell time and cost per load. Agree on them before Dallas has habits of its own.

  3. 3 Role change Seen September 3

    You started as VP Operations in September

    You joined Brightwave as VP Operations in early September. A move like that was reported on September 3.3

    What it usually means. The first months in an operations seat are usually when next year's tooling and headcount get decided. What is written down before the budget closes tends to be what gets funded.

    One idea. Draw a one-page map of where operations time goes today, by task, before the plan is final. It turns "we need more people" into "we need these two things fixed".

I put these three together because they landed within weeks of each other, and together they point to a company adding people and a second site faster than its routines can follow. Nothing here needs an answer. If it is useful, reply and tell me which part. If it is not, tell me and I will stop writing.
