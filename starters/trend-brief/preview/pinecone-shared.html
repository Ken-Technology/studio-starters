Trend 01

One automation company in eight is hiring fast

Hiring surge, 3 months

How we countedIndustrial automation companies that added staff fast in the last three monthsFrom company profiles and hiring data, counted on the day this brief was filled.

What the count says

1,120 of the 9,480 industrial automation companies counted added staff fast in the last three months, about 12 percent of the industry. That is the largest share of the three counts in this brief. A hiring surge usually means an order book that outgrew the team, on the shop floor or in the field.

What it means for Pinecone Robotics

Pinecone opened a second assembly line in August, so it knows this pressure from the inside. The other 1,120 are hiring the same controls engineers and field technicians in the same cities. If the second line needs more hands, the window to hire before those firms do is this quarter, not next.

Trend 02

New companies keep entering the field

New entrants, 2 years

7%of 9,480 companies in industrial automation

How we countedIndustrial automation companies founded in the last two yearsFrom company profiles and hiring data, counted on the day this brief was filled.

What the count says

640 industrial automation companies were founded in the last two years, about 7 percent of the industry. Each one is younger than Pinecone's per-pick subscription model. A steady flow of new entrants changes how warehouse buyers compare vendors and how patient they are on price.

What it means for Pinecone Robotics

Some of the 640 will pitch the same mid-size warehouses as Pinecone, with newer demos and no installed base to defend. The per-pick subscription and the field service teams in three states are the things a two-year-old company cannot copy quickly. Those belong at the front of the pitch, not the end.

Trend 03

Funding is flowing to a small group of automation firms

Raised money, 6 months

3%of 9,480 companies in industrial automation

How we countedIndustrial automation companies that announced funding in the last six monthsFrom company profiles and hiring data, counted on the day this brief was filled.

What the count says

310 of the 9,480 industrial automation companies announced funding in the last six months, about 3 percent of the industry. It is the smallest count in this brief. A funded competitor can underprice a subscription for a year or two, so even 310 names matter when the buyer is the same warehouse operator.

What it means for Pinecone Robotics

For Pinecone the 310 are two lists at once. Some are competitors who will discount to win mid-size warehouses while the money lasts. Others are potential partners or acquirers who need a picking robot that already works in the field. Sorting the list by what each company builds is a one-afternoon job worth doing.

What to do with this

Where I would start

Act on the hiring count first. 1,120 companies hiring fast is both the tightest labor market for the second assembly line and the clearest sign that automation buyers are spending. The new entrants and the funded group matter for the pitch, not the plan, so review them before the next trade show rather than this week.
